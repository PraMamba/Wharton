## Detailed Plot Descriptions

### 1. Home Win Rate: Regulation vs Overtime

**File**: `home_win_ot_reg.png`
**Chart type**: Vertical bar chart with bootstrap error bars

**What it shows**: Compares the home team win rate in regulation games versus overtime games, testing the hypothesis that home-ice advantage collapses in overtime.

**Visual encodings**:
- **X-axis**: Two categories — "Regulation" and "Overtime (OT)"
- **Y-axis**: Home win rate (%), zoomed to [~45%, ~65%] to amplify the signal rather than showing the full 0-100% range
- **Bar color**: Diverging `RdBu_r` colormap via `TwoSlopeNorm(vmin=0.45, vcenter=0.5, vmax=0.65)` — bars shift toward blue when home win rate exceeds 50% (home advantage) and toward red when below
- **Error bars**: 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 resamples, seeded at 42), styled in gray with cap size 6

**Key visual elements**:
- **50% reference line** (dotted gray): "Coin flip" baseline
- **58% reference line** (dashed green): "Typical league norm" for regulation home advantage
- **Red double-headed arrow** between bars: quantifies the gap (Delta) with a boxed annotation
- **Value labels**: Bold percentage + italic sample size `(n=N)` above each error bar
- **Chi-square test result** (top-right box): p-value with significance stars (`***` < 0.001, `**` < 0.01, `*` < 0.05, `ns` otherwise), colored green if significant
- **Effect size annotation** (top-center): Cohen's h value with interpretation (negligible/small/medium/large), plus odds ratio

**Statistical methods**: Bootstrap proportion CI (10,000 resamples, percentile method), Pearson chi-square test of independence on 2x2 contingency table, Cohen's h effect size, odds ratio.

**How to interpret**: Compare how far each bar sits above the 50% coin-flip line. If the CI of the OT bar overlaps 50%, overtime outcomes are statistically indistinguishable from a coin flip. The chi-square p-value and Cohen's h together tell whether the regulation-vs-OT difference is both statistically reliable and practically meaningful. Domain knowledge predicts OT home win ~52% vs regulation ~58%.

**Data source**: Raw line-level data (`whl_2025.csv`) aggregated per `game_id` by summing goals and taking `went_ot` flag. Split into regulation (`went_ot == 0`) and overtime (`went_ot == 1`) subsets.

### 2. Line Disparity vs Composite Strength

**File**: `line_disparity_scatter.png`
**Chart type**: Scatter plot with OLS trend line and 95% confidence band

**What it shows**: Tests whether balanced offensive lines (1st-line and 2nd-line producing similarly) correlate with overall team quality.

**Visual encodings**:
- **X-axis**: Adjusted line disparity ratio — 1st-line xG/60 divided by 2nd-line xG/60 (1.0 = perfect balance)
- **Y-axis**: Composite team strength score (z-score)
- **Point color**: Disparity ratio mapped through `PiYG` (colorblind-safe) diverging colormap centered at 1.0 — green = balanced, purple = imbalanced
- **Point size**: Fixed 120, white edge
- **Trend line**: OLS linear fit in blue (lw=2.2)
- **Shaded gray band**: Analytic 95% confidence interval for the mean response, computed from the design matrix: `t * sqrt(MSE * diag(X_pred @ inv(X'X) @ X_pred'))`

**Key visual elements**:
- **Orange dashed vertical line** at x=1.0: "Perfect balance (1:1)" reference
- **Team labels**: Top 5 (dark navy, bold) and bottom 3 (dark red) teams by composite score, with `adjustText` collision avoidance
- **Colorbar**: "Disparity Ratio" on right
- **Legend**: Three entries explaining high disparity, balanced, and linear fit
- **Grid**: Ultra-subtle at alpha=0.08

**Statistical methods**: OLS linear regression, Pearson r and p-value (`scipy.stats.pearsonr`), R-squared from residuals, analytic 95% CI using t-distribution with n-2 degrees of freedom.

**How to interpret**: A flat trend line with near-zero R-squared means line balance has no systematic relationship with team quality. A positive slope would suggest deeper teams win more. Teams far right of x=1.0 are top-line dependent; teams near x=1.0 have balanced scoring depth.

**Data source**: Merges `power_rankings.csv` with `line_disparity.csv` on `team`, keeping `adj_ratio` and `composite`.

### 3. Power Rankings (Top 10 + Bottom 5)

**File**: `power_rankings.png`
**Chart type**: Horizontal stacked bar chart

**What it shows**: Visualizes the top 10 and bottom 5 teams by composite strength, decomposed into the five component metrics that define the ranking.

**Visual encodings**:
- **Y-axis**: 15 teams sorted ascending by composite (best at top)
- **X-axis**: Composite strength score (z-score)
- **Stacked bar segments** (Morandi palette, each z-scored and weighted):
  - BT Strength (40%) — `#6A9EC0` steel blue
  - xGD/60 (30%) — `#7EAE8B` sage green
  - xG Share (10%) — `#D4A574` warm sand
  - Win% (10%) — `#9B8DB7` soft lavender
  - PDO (10%) — `#C4807A` dusty rose
- **Bar height**: 0.50
- **Triangle markers**: Up-triangle (green) or down-triangle (red) for teams with |z| > 1.96 (p < 0.05 two-tailed significance)

**Key visual elements**:
- **Dashed gray vertical line** at league median composite score
- **Dotted gray line** at x=0
- **Blue shaded bands**: +/-1 SD (alpha=0.05) and +/-2 SD (alpha=0.03) background zones
- **Value labels**: Composite score to 2 decimal places, positioned outside each bar
- **Component legend** above: 5 entries with weights

**Statistical methods**: Z-score normalization with `ddof=1` (sample std for 32-team population), two-tailed z-test using |z| > 1.96 threshold for significance markers.

**How to interpret**: The total bar width equals the composite z-score. Colored segments reveal which metrics drive each team's ranking — e.g., a team with a long blue segment (BT Strength) excels at the model's primary predictor. Triangle markers flag statistically elite or cellar-level teams. The +/-1 SD and +/-2 SD shading provides population context.

**Data source**: `power_rankings.csv`. Top 10 by `.nlargest(10, "composite")`, bottom 5 by `.nsmallest(5, "composite")`.

### 4. Round 1 Matchup Predictions

**File**: `round1_predictions.png`
**Chart type**: Horizontal bar chart with graduated CI overlays and tier separators

**What it shows**: Presents home win probability for each Round 1 matchup, grouped by competitiveness tier.

**Visual encodings**:
- **Y-axis**: Matchup labels ("Home Team vs Away Team")
- **X-axis**: Home win probability (%), formatted with `PercentFormatter`
- **Bar color**: `RdBu_r` colormap through `TwoSlopeNorm(vmin=0.3, vcenter=0.5, vmax=0.7)` — blue = home favored, red = away favored
- **CI overlay**: Horizontal lines spanning [CI_lo, CI_hi] with graduated styling — line width scales with uncertainty: `lw = 1.2 + 2.5 * min(ci_width / 0.3, 1.0)`, alpha scales inversely. Cap markers at endpoints
- **Probability labels**: Bold if confidence > 0.3 (probability outside 35-65%)

**Key visual elements**:
- **Yellow contested zone** (axvspan 45-55%, alpha=0.18): Toss-up region
- **Gray dashed line** at 50%
- **Tier separators**: Horizontal gray lines between Toss-up, Competitive, and Lopsided groups
- **Rotated tier labels** on right side: "Toss-up" (45-55%), "Competitive" (35-45% or 55-65%), "Lopsided" (outside 35-65%)
- **Expected upset count** in subtitle: Sum of underdog win probabilities across all matchups
- **Legend**: Home Favored (blue), Away Favored (red), 95% Bootstrap CI (gray)

**How to interpret**: Each bar's position relative to the 50% line shows which team is favored. CI width represents bootstrap uncertainty from 200-resample BT procedure. Games are sorted within tiers by probability, then by tier — Toss-ups first, then Competitive, then Lopsided.

**Data source**: `matchup_predictions.csv` with `home_win_prob`, `prob_ci_lo`, `prob_ci_hi` columns.

### 7. Brier Score & Log-Loss Comparison

**File**: `brier_logloss.png`
**Chart type**: Side-by-side horizontal lollipop charts (dual-panel, shared y-axis)

**What it shows**: Compares model calibration quality using two proper scoring rules.

**Visual encodings**:
- **Y-axis** (shared): Model names (shortened at first parenthesis)

**Key visual elements**:
- **Value labels**: 4 decimal places, bold for best/worst
- **Stems start at the best value** (not zero), amplifying differences between models
- **Shared legend** at top: Best (green), Worst (red), Other (blue)
- **Suptitle**: "Model Calibration Comparison"

**How to interpret**: Both metrics reward calibrated probabilities — lower is better. Brier score penalizes squared probability error; log-loss penalizes log probability error (more sensitive to confident wrong predictions). ### 10. Team Playing Style Archetypes (PCA Biplot)

**File**: `pca_biplot.png`
**Chart type**: PCA biplot (scores + loadings) with K-means cluster ellipses

**What it shows**: Projects teams into a 2D playing-style space defined by the first two principal components, then clusters them into three stylistic archetypes.

**Visual encodings**:
- **X-axis**: PC1 with explained variance percentage
- **Y-axis**: PC2 with explained variance percentage
- **Team points**: Colored by K-means cluster assignment (k=3), using Morandi palette (`#6A9EC0`, `#7EAE8B`, `#D4A574`). Size=110, white edge
- **Cluster ellipses**: 95% confidence ellipses from within-cluster covariance eigendecomposition; width/height = 2 x 2.0 x sqrt(eigenvalue); filled at alpha=0.08, dashed edge
- **Loading arrows** (Gabriel biplot): Arrows from origin to `corr_loadings * arrow_scale`, where `corr_loadings = loadings * sqrt(explained_variance)`. Linewidth proportional to loading magnitude: `lw = 0.8 + 2.0 * (mag / max_mag)`. Labels offset by 1.14x arrow tip
- **Correlation circle**: Dashed gray circle of radius `arrow_scale = score_range * 0.75`
- **Team labels**: 8 teams — 4 PC extremes (argmax/argmin on each PC) plus additional Mahalanobis outliers
- **Dotted gray crosshair** at (0, 0)

**Feature set** (7 features, redundant `ev_xgd60` and `xg_share` excluded):
- xGD/60, PIM/G, Depth Gap, PP xGF/60, PK xGA/60, GSAx/60, Pen Diff/G

**Cluster naming**: Dynamically derived from top 2 most-influential features per cluster (by absolute z-score mean), mapped through a style dictionary (e.g., "Offensive / Disciplined").

**How to interpret**: Teams that cluster together share similar multi-dimensional profiles. Arrow direction shows which features load most strongly on each PC; arrow length near the correlation circle means the feature is well-explained by the 2D projection. Teams near an arrow tip are extreme on that feature. Cross-arrows reveal independent dimensions (e.g., goalie quality vs offensive quality).

**Statistical methods**: PCA (2 components), StandardScaler, K-means clustering (k=3, n_init=10), eigendecomposition for cluster confidence ellipses, Mahalanobis distance for outlier labeling.

**Data source**: `power_rankings.csv`, rows with NaN dropped.

### 11. Prediction Uncertainty (Bootstrap Ridgeline)

**File**: `bootstrap_ridgeline.png`
**Chart type**: Ridgeline (joy plot) with gradient fills and credible interval overlays

**What it shows**: The full bootstrap distribution of BT strength differences for each Round 1 matchup, revealing prediction uncertainty.

**Visual encodings**:
- **X-axis**: BT Strength Difference (Home minus Away)
- **Y-axis**: No tick labels; each row is one matchup, spaced 1.0 unit apart
- **Row sorting**: By `|home_win_prob - 0.5|` ascending — most uncertain in center
- **Ridge fill**: Gaussian KDE (bandwidth=0.25) over 200 bootstrap BT difference samples. Scaled to `spacing * 1.6` for cinematic overlap effect
- **Fill color**: `RdBu_r` via `TwoSlopeNorm(vmin=0.3, vcenter=0.5, vmax=0.7)` — blue=home favored, red=away favored
- **Gradient fill**: 18 horizontal bands, each darker at base than tip. Alpha: `base_alpha * (1.0 - band/18 * 0.6)`, where `base_alpha = 0.45 + 0.45 * |prob - 0.5| * 2` (more certain = more saturated)
- **Ridge outline**: 65% brightness of fill color, lw=1.6
- **White CI overlays**: 50% CI (Q25-Q75, alpha=0.22) and 90% CI (Q05-Q95, alpha=0.10) as white `fill_between` bands on the ridge
- **Median line**: Vertical `vlines(Q50)` in dark color (lw=2.0) with boxed `{Q50:+.2f}` annotation

**Key visual elements**:
- **Alternating row shading**: Every other row light gray (alpha=0.5) for readability
- **Yellow contested zone**: Vertical band at x in [-0.15, 0.15] (alpha=0.22) — BT diff near zero
- **Left labels**: Matchup name (bold) + `P(Home) = X.X%` (smaller, blue/red by direction)
- **Right labels**: "Home Favored" (green), "Away Favored" (red), or "Contested" (orange), based on whether the 90% CI straddles zero
- **Legend**: 50% CI, 90% CI, and Median

**How to interpret**: A narrow, tall ridge = high bootstrap agreement = confident prediction. A wide, flat ridge = high uncertainty. Ridges entirely to the right of x=0 indicate home dominance across all bootstrap samples. The 50% CI overlay (whitened center) shows the interquartile range. Ridges straddling x=0 are true toss-ups.

**Statistical methods**: Bootstrap BT model (200 resamples), Gaussian KDE (`scipy.stats.gaussian_kde`, bw=0.25), percentile credible intervals (Q05, Q25, Q50, Q75, Q95).

**Data source**: Raw `whl_2025.csv` through `build_game_level()` then `bootstrap_bt(n_boot=200)`. Per-matchup BT difference: `samples[:, home_idx] - samples[:, away_idx]`.

### 12. Team Clustering by Playing Style (Dendrogram)

**File**: `dendrogram.png`
**Chart type**: Hierarchical clustering dendrogram (Ward linkage)

**What it shows**: Which teams are playing-style neighbors based on multi-dimensional feature similarity.

**Visual encodings**:
- **X-axis**: Team names (leaf labels), rotated 90 degrees
- **Y-axis**: Ward linkage distance (merge cost at each step)
- **Tree branches**: Gray (`above_threshold_color = C_GRAY`)
- **Leaf label color**: Composite strength quartile (`pd.qcut` into 4 bins)
  - Q4 (strongest 25%): Green, bold
  - Q3: Blue, normal
  - Q2: Orange, normal
  - Q1 (weakest 25%): Red, normal
- **Legend**: Four quartile entries as colored squares

**Feature set** (9 features): xGD/60, xG Share, PIM/G, Depth Gap, EV xGD/60, PP xGF/60, PK xGA/60, GSAx/60, Pen Diff/G

**How to interpret**: Teams that merge early (at low Ward distance) are playing-style neighbors in 9-dimensional feature space. If a cluster mixes green (Q4) and red (Q1) leaves, playing style does not determine success in that group. Major bifurcations (high-level splits) reveal the broadest stylistic divides in the league.

**Statistical methods**: Ward linkage (minimizes within-cluster variance), StandardScaler normalization, quartile binning via `pd.qcut`.

**Data source**: `power_rankings.csv`, columns filtered by existence and rows by NaN.

### 15. Feature Partial Correlation Network

**File**: `feature_network.png`
**Chart type**: Network graph with circular hierarchical layout and Bezier edge bundling

**What it shows**: Direct (partial) correlations between 13 hockey metrics, controlling for all other features simultaneously.

**Visual encodings**:
- **Nodes**: 13 metrics arranged in a circle, grouped by category with angular gaps between groups. Node size scales with degree centrality: `1600 + 1200 * (degree / max_degree)`
- **Node colors** (Morandi palette by category):
  - Offense: `#6A9EC0` steel blue (BT, xGD/60, xG%, EV xGD)
  - Results: `#7EAE8B` sage green (Win%)
  - Special Teams: `#D4A574` warm sand (PP xGF, PK xGA, PDO)
  - Goalie: `#9B8DB7` soft lavender (GSAx/G, GSAx60)
  - Discipline/Depth: `#C4807A` dusty rose (PIM/G, PenDiff, Depth)
- **Node labels**: White text on dark nodes, dark text on light nodes (luminance-based contrast)
- **Edges**: Bezier curves bundled toward center. Only drawn when BOTH `|partial_corr| > 0.05` AND marginal Pearson p-value < Bonferroni-corrected alpha (0.05/78)
  - Solid blue (`#6A9EC0`): Positive partial correlation
  - Dashed red (`#C4807A`): Negative partial correlation
  - Width: `1.0 + partial_r * 8.0`
  - Alpha: `0.3 + 0.6 * min(weight / 0.4, 1.0)`
- **Background**: Warm off-white (`#F5F3EF`) to reduce eye strain
- **Subtitle**: Method note + number of edges retained

**Edge bundling**: Control point at `mid * (1 - bundle_strength)` where `bundle_strength = 0.15 + 0.55 * (angular_distance / pi)` — distant nodes' edges curve more toward center.

**How to interpret**: Edges represent partial correlations — associations remaining after controlling for all other features. This is more informative than raw correlation because it removes spurious correlations from common causes. Blue solid = positive after controlling for everything else. Dashed red = negative. Isolated nodes have no significant partial associations. Large nodes (high degree centrality) are "hub" features.

**Statistical methods**: GraphicalLassoCV (5-fold CV, L1-regularized precision matrix), partial correlation from precision matrix (`-Theta_ij / sqrt(Theta_ii * Theta_jj)`), Pearson correlation for marginal significance, Bonferroni correction (alpha = 0.05 / 78 for 13 features).

**Data source**: `power_rankings.csv`, NaN rows dropped, StandardScaler applied.

### 17. Goalie Workload vs Performance

**File**: `goalie_contour.png`
**Chart type**: Scatter plot with interpolated filled contour background

**What it shows**: How goalie workload (shot pressure faced on PK) interacts with performance (goals saved above expected).

**Visual encodings**:
- **X-axis**: `pk_xga60` — PK expected goals against per 60 minutes (workload proxy)
- **Y-axis**: `goalie_gsax60` — goals saved above expected per 60 (performance, positive = better)
- **Contour background**: Cubic interpolation of composite strength onto 80x80 grid, rendered as `contourf` with 12 levels, `coolwarm` colormap (alpha=0.3)
- **Scatter points**: Colored by composite via `viridis` (s=90, white edge)
- **Colorbar**: "Composite Strength"
- **Team labels**: Top 3 and bottom 3 by `goalie_gsax60`, collision-resolved
- **Dotted gray horizontal line** at y=0: Break-even goalie performance

**Four quadrants**:
- **Top-right**: High workload, high performance — goalie carrying a battered PK unit
- **Top-left**: Low workload, high performance — goalie thriving under low pressure
- **Bottom-right**: High workload, poor performance — the danger zone
- **Bottom-left**: Low workload, poor performance — weak goalie masked by disciplined team

**How to interpret**: The y=0 line separates goalies who outperform expectations (above) from underperformers (below). The contour background smoothly reveals whether composite team strength concentrates in specific quadrants.

**Statistical methods**: Cubic spatial interpolation via `scipy.interpolate.griddata` (purely visual, no statistical test).

**Data source**: `power_rankings.csv` — columns `pk_xga60`, `goalie_gsax60`, `composite`.
